# SPOILER · Reading habits survey plan

## Research purpose
Understand how people read fiction, whether they have shared a book with a friend, how they coordinate progress and how they handle reactions and spoilers.

The survey is an initial screener and a way to identify people for follow-up interviews. It does not replace the required 3–5 interviews.

## Target participants
- People approximately 18–30 who read fiction.
- Include both people who have already read a book with a friend and people who would like to try it.
- Aim to identify 3–5 suitable interview participants.

## Suggested questions
1. How often do you read fiction? (Daily / a few times a week / weekly / less often)
2. What format do you usually use? (Printed book / e-reader / phone or tablet app / audiobook / varies)
3. Have you ever read the same book as a friend? (Yes / No, but I would like to / No, not interested)
4. If yes, how did you coordinate your reading? (Open answer)
5. Did you read at the same pace or separately? (Together / separately at a similar pace / separately at different paces / not applicable)
6. How did you share reactions or discuss the book? (In person / messaging / calls / social media / notes or highlights / did not discuss / other)
7. Have you ever avoided discussing a book because you were worried about spoilers? (Often / sometimes / never)
8. Have you ever waited for a friend to catch up before discussing a chapter? (Often / sometimes / never / not applicable)
9. Would you prefer to share chapter reactions immediately, or reveal them only after both readers have finished the same section? (Immediately / only after both finish / depends on the book or person / unsure)
10. What would make reading the same book with a friend easier or more enjoyable? (Open answer)
11. Would you be willing to take part in a 15–20 minute interview? (Yes / No). If yes, collect contact details only through a separate, optional form and do not publish them in this repository.

## Data handling
Keep responses anonymous in project documentation. Do not publish names, contact details, face photos or recordings. Use participant codes (P1, P2, etc.) in research notes.
