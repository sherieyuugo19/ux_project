# SPOILER · Interview guide · 15 to 20 minutes

## Research topic
How two friends share a fiction-reading experience, coordinate reading progress and exchange reactions without unwanted spoilers.

Do not introduce SPOILER or explain the proposed solution before asking about the participant's real experiences. Ask about actual past behaviour, not only what they think they might do.

## Opening and consent
1. Thanks for your time. I am studying how people read fiction and share reactions with friends. There are no right or wrong answers.
2. May I take notes? I will not write down your name. Your answers will be anonymised as P1, P2, etc. You can skip a question or stop at any time.
3. Is it okay if I take written notes? Do not collect names, contact details, face photos or recordings.

## Questions
1. How often do you read fiction, and what formats do you usually use?
2. Have you ever read the same book as a friend? Tell me about the most recent time.
3. How did you decide which book to read, and how did you agree on the pace or stopping points?
4. Did you read at the same time or separately? How did you keep track of each other's progress?
5. When did you talk about the book? How did you avoid revealing something the other person had not read yet?
6. Tell me about a moment when your friend had finished a chapter or book before you, or you finished before them. What happened?
7. Have you ever written down a reaction, highlighted a passage or saved a thought to share later? What did you use?
8. What did you share with your friend, and through which tools or channels? Can you show me an example if you are comfortable, without showing private messages or identifying information?
9. Was there anything awkward, inconvenient or frustrating about sharing reactions? What did you do instead?
10. Have you wanted to read a book with a friend but never started? What stopped you?
11. When you share reactions, do you prefer to see them immediately, or would you rather wait until both people finish the same section? Why?
12. What would make a shared reading experience feel natural rather than like homework or a strict schedule?
13. Is there anything else about reading with a friend that I should have asked?

## Probes
- What happened next?
- Can you give me a specific example?
- How did that make you feel?
- Why did you choose that workaround?
- What would you do if your friend was reading more slowly?

## Closing
Thank you. May I contact you again through the course-approved channel to test a prototype? Keep any follow-up contact information separate from research notes.

## Research ethics
Use participant codes only (P1, P2, etc.). Do not include names, contact details, face photos or recordings in this public repository. Separate existing reading partners from people who want to start reading together when analysing the findings.

## AI use
ChatGPT · helped draft and organise interview questions. The student must conduct the interviews, take anonymised notes and analyse the findings.
