# References

## Competitors: products addressing related user needs (4)

### 1. Bookties · bookties.app
Link: https://bookties.app/ · Screenshot: img/01-bookties.png (book view with two
readers' progress and a locked note)
- What I take from it: one book view that shows both readers' progress and notes
  that stay sealed until a chapter is reached, so locked content is visible but
  unreadable.
- What I would avoid: progress is entered by hand chapter by chapter because there is
  no reader, which depends on both people keeping it honest.

### 2. Gloss: Read with Friends · App Store
Link: https://apps.apple.com/ru/app/gloss-read-with-friends/id6762062764 ·
Screenshot: img/02-gloss.png (store screenshot of the reader or note sheet)
- What I take from it: an EPUB reader where a highlight turns into a note that
  waits for a friend and opens when they reach the same page.
- What I would avoid: sharing is flexible (single note, all notes, friends, groups),
  which adds choices a two-person app does not need.

### 3. ReadWith · readwith.app
Link: https://readwith.app/ · Screenshot: img/03-readwith.png (reading thread
with a note from a reader further ahead)
- What I take from it: a rule that you see your partner's notes only up to the point
  where you wrote your own, so a thought comes before the other person's opinion.
- What I would avoid: it runs only in a browser with pages entered by hand and no
  built-in chat, so the discussion moves to another app.

### 4. The StoryGraph, Buddy Reads · thestorygraph.com
Link: https://thestorygraph.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/79000141943-buddy-reads-and-readalongs-on-the-storygraph
Screenshot: img/04-storygraph.png (buddy read comments with progress bar)
- What I take from it: comments anchored to a position in the book, shown with a
  progress marker above each comment.
- What I would avoid: users report that comments get out of sync when readers use
  different formats, and that they had to update their own progress to see them.

## UI/UX patterns: one interaction that can inform the journey (3)

### 5. BeReal · "post to see" lock
Link: https://help.bereal.com/hc/articles/7350386715165 · Screenshot:
img/05-bereal.png (blurred feed before posting)
- The pattern: content stays blurred until you add your own, and a late post is
  labelled instead of blocked.
- Why it fits my app: it is the closest known example of "contribute first, then see".
  I would avoid the timed pressure, because a forced note would weaken the reveal.

### 6. Letterboxd · Diary and Lists
Link: https://letterboxd.com/ · Screenshot: img/06-letterboxd.png (profile diary,
film activity, or a curated list)
- The pattern: a personal diary and lists that turn individual experiences into
  an organized history, with each entry connected to a specific film.
- Why it fits my app: SPOILER could use a similar approach for a Shared Journal,
  allowing two readers to preserve favorite quotes, reactions, and memories from
  each book.
- What I would avoid: a public social feed, follower-focused interactions, and
  ratings that could distract from the private two-person reading experience.

### 7. Apple Books · text selection menu
Link: https://support.apple.com/en-kg/guide/iphone/read-books-iphc1af7c57/14.0 ·
Screenshot: img/07-apple-books.png (selection with the Highlight / Note menu)
- The pattern: touch and hold a word, adjust the selection, then choose Highlight or
  Note from a short menu.
- Why it fits my app: iOS readers already know this gesture, so I do not need to
  invent one (Jakob's law). Nothing here connects a note to a shared checkpoint,
  which is exactly the part I would add.
